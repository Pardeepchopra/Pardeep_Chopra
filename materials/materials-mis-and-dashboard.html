Materials MIS & Dashboard is an industrial materials-management discipline concerned with controlling the relevant material, information, transaction, cost, service and risk dimensions in a systematic manner.

Materials MIS & Dashboard should be understood as an operating discipline rather than as an isolated activity. Its quality depends on the interaction of physical material, information, transactions, people, equipment, suppliers and decisions. The practical objective is to create a controlled flow in which the required material outcome is achieved with appropriate service, cost, accuracy, safety and traceability.

1Fundamental

The fundamental idea is to control the variables that can change the outcome of Materials MIS & Dashboard. These normally include quantity, timing, identity, condition, location, ownership/status, demand, supply, cost and information quality. A strong process makes these variables visible, defines what is acceptable, establishes what happens when the result falls outside the expected condition, and ensures that the exception is not simply passed to the next department.

2Meaning and Operating Context

In industrial practice, Materials MIS & Dashboard sits within an interconnected materials cycle. A decision made in one function can change inventory, purchase workload, production continuity, maintenance readiness, working capital or ERP accuracy elsewhere. The topic therefore has to be interpreted in the context of the complete material flow rather than only the activity performed by one department.

The correct operating context is determined by material characteristics, demand behavior, consequence of non-availability, replenishment lead time, storage conditions, transaction architecture, supplier capability and the level of control justified by risk.

3Principle

  • Control the cause before correcting the symptom.
  • Use the simplest control that reliably prevents or detects the important failure mode.
  • Make the physical and system status of material agree as closely and as quickly as practical.
  • Use differentiated control where material value, criticality, variability or consequence differs materially.
  • Close the feedback loop by measuring results and changing the process when evidence supports improvement.

4Objectives and Purpose

  • Convert operational activity into a small set of measurable, decision-useful indicators.
  • Detect deterioration early rather than waiting for shortages, excess stock, delayed supply or accounting differences.
  • Create a common calculation basis so different departments interpret performance consistently.
  • Link the metric to an owner, target, review frequency and corrective-action mechanism.

5Importance and Significance

Effective Materials MIS & Dashboard contributes to operational continuity because materials are rarely independent of production, maintenance, quality, finance and customer commitments. When the discipline is strong, the organization can identify what is required, act before a constraint becomes critical, and make decisions using consistent information.

The significance of poor control is equally important. Failures can appear as shortages, excess inventory, obsolete stock, duplicate purchasing, unplanned expediting, incorrect valuation, lost material, production interruption, maintenance delay, supplier conflict or misleading management reports. The visible symptom may occur in a different department from the original cause.

6Characteristics of a Well-Controlled Process

  • Defined scope and clear process boundary.
  • Identifiable inputs, outputs and decision points.
  • Traceable records or transactions appropriate to the activity.
  • Explicit ownership and escalation for exceptions.
  • Measurable performance rather than purely subjective judgment.
  • Ability to operate consistently during routine and abnormal conditions.

8Need in Industry

Industrial environments require Materials MIS & Dashboard because material decisions are exposed to uncertainty, physical constraints and commercial consequences. Demand can change, suppliers can delay, material can deteriorate, specifications can be misunderstood and transactions can be posted late or incorrectly. A defined method reduces dependence on memory and individual judgment while still allowing expert intervention for abnormal cases.

9Methodologies and Practical Approaches

  • Define the process boundary and the business outcome before selecting controls.
  • Segment items, transactions or suppliers by value, criticality, variability and risk instead of applying one rule to everything.
  • Use standard work for routine activities and an explicit exception path for abnormal conditions.
  • Use trend analysis and ageing to identify problems before they become operational failures.

10Underlying Theories and Concepts

Control-and-feedback principle: A useful materials process operates as a feedback loop: a requirement or trigger creates an action, the result is measured, deviations are investigated, and the process parameters are adjusted where evidence shows that change is required.

11Quantitative Measures

Where numerical control is useful for Materials MIS & Dashboard, define a stable unit of measure, period, population and calculation boundary. Typical measurements include quantity, value, elapsed time, frequency, percentage, rate, variance, ageing, utilization, availability or compliance. The selected measure should answer a management question rather than exist only because the data is available.

14General Problems and Practical Solutions

Common problem Practical solution
Inconsistent execution between shifts or departments Define standard work, decision rules and minimum transaction requirements; train users and review exceptions.
Incomplete or inaccurate data Make critical fields mandatory, validate entries, reconcile source records and assign data ownership.
Late identification of exceptions Use trigger-based alerts, ageing reports, exception queues and defined escalation times.
Local optimization at the expense of the end-to-end process Measure the complete material flow and include downstream effects in decisions.
No closure of recurring issues Assign an accountable owner, due date, root-cause method and effectiveness check.

15Optimization Techniques

  • Remove unnecessary handoffs and duplicate data entry.
  • Use exception-based management so scarce expert time is focused on high-risk deviations.
  • Segment controls according to value, criticality, variability and consequence instead of imposing identical controls on every item.
  • Automate repeatable calculations, alerts and reconciliations where data quality is sufficient.
  • Review parameters from actual performance and change them through controlled decisions rather than informal overrides.

16Advancements and Modern Practices

Modern industrial practice increasingly connects Materials MIS & Dashboard with ERP transactions, barcode or RFID identification where justified, mobile execution, analytics, exception dashboards, automated alerts, supplier collaboration and controlled master-data workflows. The technology should be selected after the process is understood; automation of a poorly defined process can simply make errors occur faster.

  • Use event-based alerts for approaching due dates, unusual consumption, ageing, variance or control failure.
  • Use integrated master data so the same item, supplier, unit, location and status are interpreted consistently.
  • Use historical data to identify recurring exceptions, seasonal behavior and parameter drift.
  • Use workflow approval and electronic evidence for decisions that require authorization.

17Safety and Compliance

  • Use safe handling practices appropriate to the material, packaging, equipment and storage environment.
  • Maintain clear segregation where material status, hazard, ownership, quality disposition or destination requires it.
  • Do not bypass mandatory inspection, authorization or transaction controls merely to accelerate the process.

18Ethics and Professional Conduct

  • Use objective facts and complete information when making or recording material decisions.
  • Do not manipulate quantities, dates, specifications, performance measures or records to make a result appear better than it is.
  • Separate legitimate commercial confidentiality from concealment of relevant facts.
  • Declare conflicts of interest and follow the organization's authorization and segregation-of-duty requirements.
  • Treat suppliers, internal users and control functions consistently and document material deviations.

19Risk, Errors and Mistakes

  • Wrong material, wrong quantity or wrong transaction can propagate into production, maintenance, finance and planning.
  • Poor data can produce apparently precise but operationally incorrect decisions.
  • Weak segregation of duties can create unauthorized purchases, adjustments or master-data changes.
  • Over-control can create delays; under-control can create loss, shortage or non-compliance. Controls should therefore be risk-based.
  • Emergency actions can bypass normal checks; the emergency path should therefore contain minimum mandatory controls and retrospective review.

A practical error-control method is to distinguish human error, process weakness, master-data error, transaction error, equipment/system failure and deliberate non-compliance. Each category requires a different response. Repeated mistakes should trigger root-cause analysis and process redesign rather than repeated reminders alone.

20Detailed Industrial Case Study

Case: improving Materials MIS & Dashboard in an industrial plant

Situation: The plant has recurring issues associated with Materials MIS & Dashboard. Different users apply different practices, records are not always synchronized and exceptions are handled reactively. The immediate management concern is the visible operational effect, while the deeper issue is the absence of a consistent control loop.

Diagnosis: The team first defines the process boundary, identifies the material and information flows, collects representative transactions, and separates normal variation from abnormal exceptions. It then identifies where a requirement is created, where information changes hands, where a transaction is posted, and where the result is verified.

Intervention: A standard operating method is established, critical master-data fields are cleaned, decision triggers are defined, and exception ownership is assigned. Staff are trained on the reason for each control rather than only on the sequence of clicks or paperwork. The first review cycle concentrates on the highest-consequence exceptions.

Control: Management reviews the selected KPIs, open exceptions, ageing and repeat failures. Where an exception repeats, the team examines the underlying cause. The process is considered stable only when performance improves without creating an unacceptable deterioration elsewhere.

Result expected: The mature state is not simply a higher KPI. It is a process in which material status is visible, decisions occur before critical dates, records are trustworthy, abnormal cases are escalated and corrective actions remain effective after the immediate problem has disappeared.

21Practical Examples

  • A dashboard shows improving purchase cycle time but worsening vendor OTIF. Management avoids treating the first metric as a complete success and examines the end-to-end effect on availability.
  • An apparent inventory accuracy decline is traced to delayed transaction posting rather than physical loss, leading to a process correction rather than a blanket stock adjustment.

22Review and Control Checklist

  • Is the scope and purpose of Materials MIS & Dashboard clearly defined?
  • Are inputs, outputs, decision points and responsibilities documented?
  • Are critical master-data and transaction fields complete and accurate?
  • Is there a defined method for exceptions, escalation and closure?
  • Are performance measures calculated consistently and reviewed at an appropriate frequency?
  • Are recurring deviations subjected to root-cause analysis rather than repeated correction?
  • Are records retained sufficiently to reconstruct important decisions and transactions?

23Responsibilities

Role Core responsibility
Stores Execute physical controls, identification, location discipline, receipt/issue/transfer transactions and record accuracy within the assigned scope.
Purchase Manage sourcing, commercial terms, supplier commitments and order actions relevant to the topic.
Planning Define or communicate requirements, dates, quantities, priorities and planning parameters.
User Department Define technical need, confirm usage/acceptance where applicable and report abnormal consumption or shortages.
Finance / Cost Control Control valuation, commercial evidence and financial impacts within its mandate.
Management Set policy, approve thresholds, review performance and remove systemic constraints.

24Limitations and Conditions

  • No methodology can compensate for fundamentally incorrect inputs; data quality remains a limiting factor.
  • Thresholds and parameters are context-dependent and should not be treated as universal constants.
  • A metric can be improved mechanically while the underlying business outcome deteriorates if the metric boundary is poorly designed.
  • Local conditions such as supplier market structure, equipment criticality, storage constraints and ERP configuration can materially affect the appropriate method.

25Management Review Questions

  • What has changed in the operating environment since the current Materials MIS & Dashboard method was established?
  • Which exceptions are increasing, recurring or remaining open beyond the expected closure time?
  • Is the measured result improving because the process improved, or because the measurement boundary changed?
  • Are controls proportionate to material value, criticality, variability and business consequence?
  • What single process weakness, if removed, would prevent the largest number of recurring failures?
