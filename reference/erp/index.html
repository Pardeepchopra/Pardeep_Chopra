MANAGEMENT OVERVIEW

ERP & MIS — Management Overview

This section provides the management context before the detailed topic references: why the function exists, how it works, who owns it, how performance is measured and what professional controls matter.

Background & Need

Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) connects material, purchasing, inventory, planning, production and financial information through controlled master data and transaction processes. Management Information Systems (MIS) convert transaction data into usable decisions. ERP provides a common transaction record, while MIS helps management see trends, exceptions, performance and risks. Neither works reliably without disciplined master data and correct transactions.

Evolution of the Function

The role has expanded from transaction processing and stock administration into a cross-functional discipline using data, planning, supplier capability, digital systems, risk management and continuous improvement. The emphasis is now on total process performance and business continuity.

Working Methods

Key practices include material and supplier master governance, PR/PO/GRN transactions, stock movements, batch/serial control where applicable, MRP parameters, approvals, document flow, reporting, dashboards and reconciliation between physical and system records.

Roles & Responsibilities

Users create accurate transactions; master-data owners control definitions and parameters; functional teams own business rules; IT maintains system integrity; management defines governance, reporting and access controls.

KPIs, Targets & Review

Transaction accuracy, master-data error rate, GRN turnaround, PR-to-PO cycle time, open-document ageing, inventory accuracy, report timeliness and exception closure are useful measures.

Vision, Ethics & Governance

ERP data is a management record and must not be manipulated to conceal shortages, delays, excess stock or process failures. Access rights, approvals and audit trails should be respected.

INDUSTRIAL CASE STUDY

Illustrative Case Study — ERP Master Data Error

A plant receives a material in a different unit of measure from the unit used in the item master. Stock appears available, but the issue quantity is inconsistent with physical packs. A master-data review identifies the UOM conversion problem, corrects governance and validates opening balances. The case shows that ERP accuracy depends on business definitions as much as on software.

ERP Fundamentals

Enterprise Resource Planning integrates material, purchasing, inventory, production and financial information through common processes and controlled master data.

SAP MM Overview

SAP Materials Management supports procurement, inventory management, material master data and related material movements.

Material Master in ERP

The material master provides controlled identity, planning, purchasing, inventory and valuation attributes for material transactions.

Vendor / Supplier Master

Supplier master data provides the controlled business identity and purchasing attributes required for procurement transactions.

ERP Material Master Governance

Material master governance controls creation, modification, duplication, classification, UOM and parameter ownership.

PR to PO Process in ERP

The PR-to-PO process converts an approved requirement into a controlled purchase commitment through requisition, sourcing, evaluation and purchase order stages.

RFQ & Quotation Management in ERP

ERP sourcing records support enquiry, quotation comparison, commercial evaluation and purchasing decisions.

Purchase Order Management in ERP

Purchase orders establish the authorized supplier, material, quantity, price, delivery and commercial conditions.

Purchase Order Amendment Control

Controlled PO amendments preserve authorization, commercial traceability and purchasing history.

Goods Receipt / GRN in ERP

Goods receipt records physical receipt against an authorized supply document and updates relevant stock and accounting information.

Material Document

A material document records an inventory-relevant transaction such as receipt, issue, transfer or adjustment with traceable quantities and dates.

ERP Movement Types

Movement types classify and control inventory transactions such as goods receipt, goods issue, transfer posting and stock transfer.

ERP Stock Types

ERP stock types distinguish material availability and status, such as unrestricted, quality inspection and blocked stock.

Stock Transfer & Transfer Posting

ERP transfer processes move or reclassify stock while maintaining quantity, location and transaction traceability.

Goods Issue in ERP

Goods issue records material consumption or dispatch from inventory against the appropriate business requirement.

Reservation Management

Reservations represent planned material requirements and help protect required stock for authorized operations.

Batch Management

Batch management maintains traceability and controlled stock by production or receipt batch where required.

Serial Number Management

Serial number management provides unit-level traceability for materials requiring individual identification.

Physical Inventory in ERP

ERP physical inventory processes reconcile counted quantities with system stock through controlled count, difference analysis and adjustment.

Inventory Valuation in ERP

Inventory valuation connects material quantities with applicable valuation methods, accounting controls and material costs.

MRP in ERP

Material Requirements Planning converts demand, inventory, receipts, lead times and planning parameters into planned supply requirements.

Purchasing & Inventory Integration

Purchasing and inventory modules interact through requirements, purchase orders, receipts, stock status and material availability.

Stores Purchase Production Integration

Integrated ERP processes connect stores receipts and issues with purchasing, production, maintenance and planning requirements.

ERP Material Planning Parameters

Planning parameters such as MRP type, lead time, lot size, reorder point and safety stock influence replenishment.

ERP & MIS Architecture

MIS converts controlled ERP transactions into operational, analytical and management information.

Daily Stores MIS

Daily stores MIS provides a concise view of receipts, issues, stock status, shortages, pending transactions and exceptions.

Purchase MIS

Purchase MIS tracks requisitions, orders, pending supplies, ageing, vendor performance, price movement and purchasing cycle time.

Inventory Dashboard

An inventory dashboard combines stock value, coverage, movement, ageing, service and exception indicators.

Vendor Performance Dashboard

Vendor dashboards monitor delivery, quality, responsiveness, price and outstanding supply performance.

ERP Data Reconciliation

Reconciliation verifies agreement between physical transactions, ERP records, supporting documents and management reports.

ERP Data Quality

ERP data quality depends on accurate item identity, UOM, vendor data, transaction discipline, timely posting and controlled master-data changes.

ERP Audit Controls

ERP audit controls provide authorization, segregation of duties, document traceability, change history and transaction review.

Excel and ERP Interface

Excel remains useful for controlled analysis and MIS when its data source, formulas, ownership and reconciliation controls are defined.

ERP & MIS KPIs

ERP and MIS effectiveness can be monitored through transaction accuracy, processing time, master-data quality, reconciliation and reporting KPIs.

ERP Governance

ERP governance establishes ownership, authorization, master-data standards, workflow discipline, review mechanisms and escalation.

ERP Implementation Controls

Successful ERP implementation requires process mapping, master-data cleansing, user roles, testing, cutover control and post-go-live stabilization.
