Reorder Point

Professional reference covering ROP, lead-time demand, safety stock, inventory position and replenishment trigger logic.

1. Definition

Reorder Point (ROP) is the inventory position at which replenishment should be initiated so that expected supply arrives before the available stock becomes insufficient, subject to the selected inventory policy.

2. Basic ROP Formula

ROP = Lead-Time Demand + Safety Stock

If demand is expressed as average daily consumption:

ROP = Average Daily Consumption × Lead Time + Safety Stock

3. Worked Example

Average daily consumption = 25 units
Lead time = 8 days
Safety stock = 100 units

Lead-Time Demand = 25 × 8 = 200 units

ROP = 200 + 100

ROP = 300 units

Therefore, the defined replenishment trigger is approximately 300 units under this simplified policy.

4. Inventory Position Versus Physical Stock

In a practical ERP environment, the replenishment decision may need to consider more than physical stock.

Inventory Position = On-Hand Stock + Open / Scheduled Receipts − Allocated / Reserved Quantity

The exact inventory-position definition should follow the organization's ERP and planning policy.

5. Factors Affecting ROP

FactorImpact
Consumption rateHigher consumption generally increases lead-time demand.
Lead timeLonger lead time generally increases stock required before replenishment arrives.
Safety stockProvides protection against uncertainty.
CriticalityCritical materials may require stronger availability protection.
Supplier reliabilityVariable delivery can increase uncertainty.
Demand variabilityMay require a larger buffer.

6. ROP Decision Logic

  1. Calculate or estimate demand during lead time.
  2. Determine appropriate safety stock.
  3. Calculate ROP.
  4. Monitor inventory position.
  5. Trigger replenishment according to the defined policy.
  6. Consider open orders and expected receipts.
  7. Review parameters when demand or supply conditions change.

7. Common ROP Errors

  • Using outdated consumption data.
  • Ignoring supplier lead-time changes.
  • Using physical stock instead of inventory position where open orders matter.
  • Applying the same ROP to materials with very different demand patterns.
  • Never reviewing parameters after major process changes.
