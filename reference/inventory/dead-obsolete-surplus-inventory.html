Dead / Obsolete / Surplus Inventory

Practical framework for identifying excess, surplus, non-moving, dead and obsolete industrial inventory and controlling working-capital exposure.

1. Definitions

TermPractical Meaning
Excess InventoryInventory above the quantity reasonably required for the defined demand and control policy.
Surplus InventoryInventory beyond identified operational requirements or available demand.
Slow-Moving InventoryInventory with relatively low movement according to the organization's criteria.
Non-Moving InventoryInventory with no movement during the defined analysis period.
Obsolete InventoryInventory that is no longer required or usable for its intended application according to the organization's determination.
Dead InventoryA management term generally used for inventory with no useful movement or foreseeable requirement under the defined policy.

2. Why Dead Inventory Develops

  • Over-purchasing.
  • Change in equipment design.
  • Plant modification.
  • Specification change.
  • Project cancellation.
  • Duplicate item creation.
  • Incorrect demand forecast.
  • Minimum order quantities larger than actual requirements.
  • Procurement made without validating existing stock.
  • Equipment discontinued.
  • Technology change.

3. Identification Framework

  1. Analyze last issue and movement date.
  2. Calculate current stock value.
  3. Check future requirements.
  4. Check open work orders and maintenance plans.
  5. Check equivalent or substitute items.
  6. Check other departments or plants.
  7. Review equipment status.
  8. Classify the material according to the approved policy.

4. Action Options

SituationPossible Action
Future requirement existsRetain and review stocking quantity.
Excess quantityStop/reduce further procurement and investigate consumption.
Another department requires itTransfer where technically appropriate.
Equivalent item existsReview item-master duplication and standardization.
Supplier return possibleEvaluate commercial return opportunity.
Material no longer requiredProcess through approved surplus/obsolete disposal procedure.
Technically usable elsewhereEvaluate alternate application.

5. Financial Impact

Dead and obsolete inventory ties up working capital while also consuming storage space and management attention. It may additionally create preservation, deterioration and disposal costs.

Dead Inventory % = Dead Inventory Value ÷ Total Inventory Value × 100

6. Prevention Framework

  • Validate existing stock before raising purchase requirements.
  • Maintain accurate item masters.
  • Use ABC/FSN/VED classification.
  • Review Min-Max parameters periodically.
  • Control MOQ-driven excess.
  • Monitor ageing continuously.
  • Close obsolete equipment-related material requirements.
  • Coordinate engineering, maintenance, stores and purchase decisions.
  • Maintain a formal dead-stock review register.
