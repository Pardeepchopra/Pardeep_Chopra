EOQ / Reorder Quantity

Professional reference covering Economic Order Quantity, ordering cost, carrying cost, MOQ and practical reorder quantity.

1. Economic Order Quantity

EOQ is a classical inventory model that determines an order quantity intended to balance relevant ordering and holding costs under specified assumptions.

2. Classical EOQ Formula

EOQ = √(2DS / H)

D = Annual demand in units
S = Ordering cost per order
H = Annual holding cost per unit

3. Worked Example

Annual demand = 12,000 units
Ordering cost = ₹500/order
Annual holding cost = ₹20/unit/year

EOQ = √[(2 × 12,000 × 500) / 20]

EOQ = √600,000

EOQ ≈ 775 units

The classical model therefore produces an EOQ of approximately 775 units.

4. Important EOQ Assumptions

  • Demand is reasonably stable over the analysis period.
  • Ordering cost is reasonably identifiable.
  • Holding cost can be estimated.
  • Supply is available under the assumed replenishment conditions.
  • The model does not automatically account for every real-world constraint.

5. EOQ Versus Practical Reorder Quantity

Industrial purchasing frequently operates under conditions that make the classical EOQ insufficient by itself.

FactorWhy It Matters
MOQSupplier may require a minimum quantity.
Pack SizeMaterial may be supplied only in defined multiples.
Storage CapacityLarge orders may exceed available space.
CriticalityCritical items may require different stocking logic.
Lead TimeLong or variable lead time can affect replenishment policy.
Price BreakQuantity discounts may alter economic comparisons.
Supplier ConstraintsProduction or dispatch constraints may affect feasible order size.

6. Reorder Quantity

A practical reorder quantity may be determined from demand, review period, lead time, MOQ, stock position and business policy.

Practical Reorder Quantity = Target Stock − Inventory Position

The exact target-stock definition depends on the organization's inventory control system.

7. EOQ and Number of Orders

Approximate Number of Orders per Year = Annual Demand ÷ Order Quantity

For the example above, using an order quantity of approximately 775 units:

12,000 ÷ 775 ≈ 15.5 orders/year

8. Management Principle

EOQ should be treated as an analytical reference point, not an automatic purchase instruction. Actual order quantity should consider operational, commercial, supply and inventory-control constraints.
