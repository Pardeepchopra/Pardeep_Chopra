Inventory Accuracy

Practical reference for physical verification, cycle counting, reconciliation, stock variance and inventory accuracy controls.

1. Definition

Inventory accuracy is the degree to which physical inventory matches the quantity and identity recorded in the inventory management system or stock records.

High inventory accuracy is essential because purchasing, production planning, maintenance and management decisions depend on reliable stock information.

2. Why Inventory Accuracy Matters

  • Prevents false stock availability.
  • Reduces unnecessary purchasing.
  • Improves material planning.
  • Supports reliable MRP/ERP information.
  • Reduces production and maintenance interruptions.
  • Improves financial inventory reporting.
  • Identifies process weaknesses in stores operations.

3. Inventory Accuracy Formula

A simple item-based accuracy measure can be expressed as:

Inventory Accuracy % = Correctly Matching Items ÷ Items Checked × 100

Organizations may instead use quantity accuracy, value accuracy or a tolerance-based methodology. The selected definition should be documented.

5. Common Causes of Variance

  • Incorrect material issue posting.
  • Receipt quantity entered incorrectly.
  • Material received but not posted.
  • Material physically moved without system transfer.
  • Wrong item code used during transaction.
  • Unit-of-measure conversion errors.
  • Unrecorded returns.
  • Uncontrolled scrap.
  • Job-work material not reconciled.
  • RGP material not tracked properly.
  • Duplicate or incorrect item masters.

6. Variance Investigation

StepAction
1Freeze or identify the stock position being investigated.
2Recount the physical quantity.
3Verify item code, description and UOM.
4Review recent receipts and issues.
5Check transfers, returns, RGP and job-work movements.
6Identify root cause.
7Correct the transaction or stock record according to authorization.
8Implement preventive action.

7. Cycle Counting Framework

A practical cycle-counting program can assign counting frequency based on value, criticality, movement and historical accuracy.

A high-value item with frequent transactions may justify more frequent verification than a low-value, stable item. Criticality can override a purely value-based frequency.

8. Inventory Accuracy Control Checklist

  • Every item has a unique code.
  • Every physical location is identified.
  • Receipts are posted promptly.
  • Issues are posted at the time of issue.
  • Returns are documented.
  • Transfers are recorded.
  • RGP and job-work balances are reconciled.
  • Scrap is segregated and recorded.
  • Cycle-count differences are investigated.
  • Authorized adjustments are documented.
