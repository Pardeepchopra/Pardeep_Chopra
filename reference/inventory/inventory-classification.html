Inventory Classification

A multi-dimensional framework for classifying industrial materials according to annual consumption value, movement, criticality, unit price, procurement difficulty and stock characteristics.

1. Why Inventory Classification Is Required

An industrial store may contain thousands of items with completely different values, consumption patterns, criticalities and procurement characteristics. Treating every item identically creates weak control over important materials and unnecessary administrative effort for low-impact items.

Inventory classification means grouping materials according to defined characteristics so that appropriate control, review frequency, stocking policy and management attention can be applied.

2. Major Classification Techniques

Technique Classification Basis Primary Use
ABC Annual consumption value Value-based control
FSN Movement / issue frequency Movement-based control
VED Criticality Operational importance
HML Unit price Unit-value control
SDE Procurement difficulty Supply-risk control
XYZ Defined stock/value variability criterion Stock-position analysis

3. ABC Analysis

ABC analysis normally classifies inventory according to annual consumption value.

Annual Consumption Value = Annual Quantity Consumed × Unit Cost
Class Typical Meaning Control Approach
A Highest cumulative consumption-value contribution Close monitoring, accurate forecasting, frequent review and management attention.
B Medium-value group Normal systematic control and periodic review.
C Lower-value group Simplified controls may be appropriate depending on operational importance.
ABC classification should be calculated from actual organizational data and the chosen cumulative-value policy. Percentage boundaries should be treated as policy parameters rather than universal laws.

4. FSN Analysis

FSN classifies items according to their movement or issue characteristics.

Class Meaning Typical Action
F — Fast Moving Frequently issued or consumed. Maintain reliable replenishment and accessible storage.
S — Slow Moving Issued less frequently. Review stock levels and future requirements.
N — Non-Moving No movement according to the defined analysis period. Investigate requirement, alternative use, transfer or disposal.

FSN criteria must be defined by the organization. For example, issue frequency, quantity issued, last issue date or a combination of these parameters can be used.

5. VED Analysis

Class Meaning Typical Consequence of Non-Availability
V — Vital Failure or absence has serious operational consequences. Potential major production, safety or operational impact.
E — Essential Important for operations but alternatives or recovery options may exist. Significant operational impact.
D — Desirable Useful but generally lower criticality. Limited operational impact.

VED is especially useful for maintenance and MRO inventory where operational criticality may be more important than purchase value.

6. HML Analysis

HML classifies items according to unit price.

Class Meaning
H — High High unit-price items.
M — Medium Medium unit-price items.
L — Low Low unit-price items.

HML can be useful for purchase authorization, security, storage controls and handling policies. Thresholds should be established according to the organization's material profile.

7. SDE Analysis

Class Meaning Typical Consideration
S — Scarce Difficult to procure. Long lead time, limited sources or supply constraints.
D — Difficult Procurement has significant difficulty. Special specifications, limited vendors or technical restrictions.
E — Easy Readily available. Multiple suppliers or short procurement lead time.

8. XYZ Analysis

XYZ classification is commonly used to analyze inventory according to the organization's defined stock-value or variability criterion. Different organizations may implement XYZ differently.

Important: The website will explicitly state the chosen XYZ method whenever an analytical tool is developed, rather than presenting one definition as universally applicable.

9. Combined Classification

One classification rarely answers every inventory-control question. Combining methods provides a stronger decision framework.

Combination Question Answered
ABC + FSN What is the item's financial importance and movement behaviour?
ABC + VED What is the item's financial importance and operational criticality?
ABC + FSN + VED Which items require the strongest combined control?
HML + VED Which high-value or critical items require special control?
SDE + VED Which critical items are also difficult to procure?

10. Industrial Decision Matrix

Situation Possible Management Response
High value + fast movement Strong replenishment and value control.
High value + non-moving Immediate review of excess, ageing and future requirement.
Low value + vital Do not reduce stock solely because financial value is low.
High value + difficult procurement Consider supply risk, lead time and strategic sourcing.
Slow moving + non-critical Review stocking requirement and possible reduction.
Critical + long lead time Evaluate safety/insurance stock requirements.
