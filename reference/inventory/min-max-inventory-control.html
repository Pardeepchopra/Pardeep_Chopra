Min-Max Inventory Control

Professional reference covering minimum level, maximum level, reorder point, safety stock and practical replenishment control.

1. Min-Max Inventory Control

The Min-Max system controls inventory using a defined minimum level and maximum level. When stock reaches the replenishment trigger, an order or replenishment quantity is generated according to the selected policy.

2. Core Parameters

ParameterMeaning
Minimum LevelLower control boundary or minimum desired stock.
Maximum LevelUpper target stock boundary.
Reorder PointTrigger at which replenishment is initiated.
Safety StockProtection against uncertainty.
Lead TimeExpected time required for replenishment.
Reorder QuantityQuantity ordered or replenished.

3. Practical Min-Max Method

A practical industrial method can establish:

Minimum Level = Lead-Time Demand + Safety Stock

Maximum Level = Minimum Level + Replenishment Quantity

The exact organization's policy may instead define minimum and maximum using average consumption, review period, lead time, MOQ, criticality or other parameters.

4. Worked Example

Average daily consumption = 20 units
Lead time = 10 days
Safety stock = 100 units
Replenishment quantity = 300 units

Minimum Level = (20 × 10) + 100 = 300 units

Maximum Level = 300 + 300 = 600 units

Under this simplified policy, the operating range is approximately 300–600 units.

5. Min-Max Decision Cycle

ConditionPossible Action
Stock above maximumReview excess and suspend unnecessary replenishment.
Stock between Min and MaxContinue normal monitoring.
Stock at/below reorder triggerInitiate replenishment according to policy.
Stock below minimumInvestigate shortage risk and expedite where justified.
No consumption for extended periodReview ageing and future requirement.

6. Inputs Required for Min-Max Planning

Item Code Average Consumption Maximum Consumption Lead Time Safety Stock MOQ Current Stock Open PO Quantity Reserved Quantity Criticality Reorder Quantity Minimum Level Maximum Level

7. Parameter Review

  • Review high-value items more closely.
  • Review parameters after major consumption changes.
  • Review supplier lead-time changes.
  • Consider seasonality.
  • Consider MOQ constraints.
  • Consider criticality and service requirements.
  • Check excess inventory before creating new orders.

8. Min-Max and ERP

In an ERP environment, Min-Max parameters may feed replenishment planning, purchase requisitions, MRP or automated planning processes depending on the system configuration.

Min-Max parameters are control settings, not permanent numbers. They should be periodically reviewed against actual consumption and supply performance.
