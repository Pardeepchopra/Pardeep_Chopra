Inventory Turnover Ratio

Professional reference covering inventory turnover ratio, average inventory, inventory days, stock coverage and working capital.

1. Definition

Inventory Turnover Ratio measures the relationship between the cost of inventory consumed or sold during a period and the average inventory maintained during that period.

2. Basic Formula

Inventory Turnover Ratio = Annual Cost of Consumption ÷ Average Inventory Value

Where:

Average Inventory = (Opening Inventory Value + Closing Inventory Value) ÷ 2

For organizations using a more detailed average-inventory methodology, monthly or periodic average values may be used.

3. Worked Example

Annual material consumption at cost = ₹1,20,00,000
Opening inventory = ₹25,00,000
Closing inventory = ₹35,00,000

Average Inventory = (25,00,000 + 35,00,000) ÷ 2

= ₹30,00,000

Inventory Turnover = 1,20,00,000 ÷ 30,00,000

= 4 times per year

4. Inventory Days

Inventory Days = 365 ÷ Inventory Turnover Ratio

For a turnover ratio of 4:

Inventory Days = 365 ÷ 4 = 91.25 days

This provides an approximate indication of the inventory investment represented relative to annual consumption.

5. Interpreting Turnover

Turnover should be interpreted in context rather than using a universal target.

SituationQuestions to Investigate
Turnover changes significantlyHas demand, inventory level, product mix or accounting basis changed?
Turnover is lowIs excess, slow-moving or obsolete inventory increasing?
Turnover is highIs stock availability still adequate?
Critical spares have low turnoverIs low movement intentional because of criticality?
Raw material turnover differs from MRO turnoverAre different inventory policies required?

6. Turnover and Working Capital

Inventory represents capital committed to materials. Monitoring turnover can help management understand whether inventory investment is changing relative to material consumption.

A higher turnover figure is not automatically desirable if it results from insufficient stock and frequent stock-outs. Likewise, lower turnover can be reasonable for strategic or critical inventory.

8. Management Review

  1. Calculate turnover consistently.
  2. Compare with previous periods.
  3. Separate inventory categories where necessary.
  4. Investigate ageing and excess stock.
  5. Check service-level and stock-out performance.
  6. Review critical/insurance spares separately.
  7. Link turnover analysis to working-capital decisions.
