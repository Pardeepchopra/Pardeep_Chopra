Physical Verification

Controlled physical counting and verification of inventory against ERP or stock records to establish quantity accuracy, identify discrepancies and strengthen inventory control.

Definition

Physical verification is the systematic physical counting, measurement, inspection or confirmation of inventory and comparison with the corresponding book or ERP balance.

Verification principlePlan → Freeze / Control Movement → Count → Record → Recount → Compare → Investigate → Approve → Adjust → Prevent Recurrence

Objectives

  • Establish physical inventory accuracy.
  • Identify shortages, excesses, wrong locations and unidentified stock.
  • Validate ERP balances and master data.
  • Detect transaction and process weaknesses.
  • Support financial and inventory reporting.
  • Provide evidence for authorized adjustments.

Verification Methods

MethodApplication
Annual physical inventoryBroad or complete inventory verification
Cycle countingContinuous verification of selected items
ABC countingHigher frequency for high-value items
Criticality countingFrequent verification of critical materials
Location countVerification of all items within a location
Blind countCounter does not see the book balance before counting

Counting Procedure

  1. Define scope and counting schedule.
  2. Issue counting instructions and responsibilities.
  3. Control material movements during the count.
  4. Prepare item and location lists.
  5. Physically count or measure stock.
  6. Record quantity, batch, serial and condition where required.
  7. Perform independent recounts for discrepancies.
  8. Compare physical and ERP balances.
  9. Investigate root causes.
  10. Authorize approved adjustments.
  11. Verify corrective actions.

Variance Analysis

Quantity Variance = Physical Quantity − Book Quantity
Value Variance = Quantity Variance × Applicable Unit Value

Variances should be investigated before adjustment. Typical causes include unposted receipts, unposted issues, wrong UOM, location errors, duplicate transactions, counting errors, damage, unauthorized movement and master-data problems.

Controls

  • Use controlled count sheets or system count documents.
  • Separate counting from approval of adjustments where practical.
  • Require recounts above defined tolerance.
  • Maintain evidence of investigation.
  • Review recurring variance by item, location, department and process.
  • Do not use routine adjustments to conceal process failures.

KPIs

Inventory Accuracy

Items or quantities matching the book balance within tolerance.

Count Completion

Planned versus completed counts.

Variance Value

Value of identified discrepancies.

Adjustment Rate

Frequency/value of authorized inventory adjustments.

Common Errors

  • Counting while uncontrolled movements continue.
  • Counting duplicate locations twice.
  • Ignoring damaged or quarantined stock.
  • Using incorrect UOM.
  • Adjusting stock without root-cause investigation.
  • Failing to distinguish timing differences from true shortages.

Excel / MIS Application

Use Item Code, Description, Location, Book Qty, Physical Qty, Variance Qty, Unit Rate, Variance Value, Batch/Serial, Counter, Recount Qty, Reason, Approval and Adjustment Document.
